Free Reselling Starter Checklist

Reselling means buying a finished product at a wholesale price and selling it to customers for a little more. This guide takes you from choosing one product to your first delivery, in five plain steps. It is free, and it is not a promise of income.

Before you start

You pay for the stock before you sell it, so only use a small amount you can afford to lose. Do not borrow money to buy stock. Nobody can tell you in advance how much you will sell.

Selling fake goods or copies of branded products is not okay. It is unfair to the customer, it can bring legal trouble, and it ends trust quickly. Sell plain, honestly described products only.

Step 1. Pick one product people already buy

Choose one small, light, low-priced product that people around you already buy often. Good first choices are phone covers, simple jewellery and stationery.

  • Small and light: it is cheap to pack and post.
  • Easy to explain: a customer should understand it from one photo.
  • Hard to break: glass and electronics cause more returns.
  • Asked for: ask five friends or classmates, "Would you buy this? How much would you pay?"

Start with one product only. You can add more later.

Step 2. Find a supplier and order a small batch first

  1. Visit a local wholesale market. If you are not sure where it is, ask a shop owner where they buy their stock.
  2. Ask for the price at three different shops and compare.
  3. Hold the product in your hand and check the quality (see the list below).
  4. Order a small batch first, for example 10 to 20 pieces. Do not buy 100 pieces before you know people want it.
  5. Keep the bill or a note of what you paid.

Step 3. Take clear photos and post a reel

  1. Take photos in daylight near a window. Do not use the flash.
  2. Put the product on a plain background, such as a white sheet or paper.
  3. Wipe the product and hold the phone steady. Take a front view, a close-up and a photo that shows the size.
  4. Make a 15 to 30 second reel that shows the product, the price and how to order. For example: "DM me to order."
  5. Show the real colour. A photo that looks better than the product leads to returns and bad reviews.

Step 4. Set your price

Your selling price must cover what you paid, your packing and delivery costs, and something left for you. Look at what other sellers charge for the same kind of product before you decide.

Example only: buy at ₹150, sell at ₹250. That is ₹100 on each piece before packing and delivery costs. If you sold 30 pieces, that would be 30 × ₹100 = ₹3,000 before those costs. Your numbers will be different, and some pieces may not sell. This is not an income promise.

Step 5. Take orders, collect payment first, send and ask for a review

  1. Take orders by Instagram DM. Reply with the price, the colours or sizes, and the delivery cost.
  2. Ask for UPI payment first. Send your UPI ID and the exact amount, and wait until the money shows as received in your UPI app.
  3. Pack the product carefully so it does not get damaged on the way.
  4. Send it and share the tracking or courier details with the customer.
  5. A few days after it arrives, ask for an honest review.

Keep a small notebook with each order: date, customer, amount paid and the delivery status.

8 product ideas to look at

These are only ideas. Check the price and quality in your own market first.

  • Phone covers

    Plain or printed covers for popular phone models. Keep a small stock for the most common models only.

  • Simple jewellery

    Earrings, bracelets and chains without a brand name. Light, easy to pack and popular as gifts.

  • Stationery sets

    Notebooks, pens, markers and sticker sheets. Students buy these again and again.

  • Hair accessories

    Clips, scrunchies and hair bands. Very cheap to buy and post.

  • Keychains and charms

    Small items that work well as add-ons or gifts.

  • Pouches and tote bags

    Pencil pouches, cloth bags and small purses without a brand logo.

  • Phone stands and accessories

    Stands, cable holders and pop grips. Check that they work before you list them.

  • Small home decor

    Wall hangings, candle holders and festival items. Sells well before festivals. Pack with care.

What to check before you buy stock

  • Quality: check the finish, stitching, colour and packing. Open a few pieces from the batch, not only the sample on display.
  • Returns: ask what happens if pieces are damaged or wrong. Ask if the shop will exchange them, and get that in writing or on the bill.
  • Genuine goods only: do not buy items with a copied brand name or logo. If a price looks too low for a "branded" item, it is probably fake.
  • Bill and GST: ask for a bill. If the supplier or your sales need GST, ask about it and check the rules for your case. If you are unsure, ask a local accountant.
  • Total cost: add transport from the market, packing material and delivery before you decide your selling price.

If a supplier refuses to show you the product or give a bill, look for another one.

Hi [name], thank you for asking! The [product] is ₹[price]. It is available in [colours or sizes].

Delivery to [city] is ₹[delivery charge] and takes about [number] days. Would you like me to keep one for you?

2. Ask for payment

Payment request

Thank you! Your order: [product] × [number] = ₹[total], including delivery.

Please pay by UPI to [your UPI ID] and send me a screenshot. I will pack and send your order as soon as I see the payment.

Please also send your name, full address with pincode and phone number.

3. Ask for a review

Review request

Hi [name], your [product] should have reached you by now. Is everything fine?

If you like it, could you reply with one honest line, or a photo? It helps me a lot. If something is wrong, tell me and I will do my best to fix it. Thank you!
