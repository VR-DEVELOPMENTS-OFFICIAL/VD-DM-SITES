Free Photo Editing Checklist for Shops

Many small shops post product photos that are too dark, crooked or messy, and good photos help them sell. If you can learn to fix photos on your phone with a free app, you can offer this as a small paid service. This guide takes you from finding your first shop to your first review, in five plain steps. It is free, and it is not a promise of income.

Before you start

Only edit photos that the shop owner gave you, and only with their permission. Do not take photos from other people's pages or from the internet.

Fix the light, the crop and the background, but do not change the product itself. Do not make the colour, size, shape or quality look different from the real thing. A customer who receives something that looks different from the photo will return it and stop trusting the shop. Nobody can tell you in advance how many shops will say yes.

Step 1. Look at what small shops post and find weak photos

Open the Instagram or WhatsApp Business pages of shops near you. Look for photos you could clearly improve, and keep a short list of five to ten shops.

  • Too dark or too bright: the product is hard to see.
  • Crooked or badly cropped: the edges are cut off, or there is a lot of empty space.
  • Messy background: clutter, shadows or other products behind it.
  • Every photo looks different: different colours and different sizes on the same page.

Pick shops that post new products often. Write down the shop name, what they sell and which photos you would fix.

Step 2. Learn a free phone app and fix light, crop and background

  1. Install one free photo editing app on your phone. Pick one that lets you change brightness, crop and straighten without a payment. Use only that one app until you are comfortable.
  2. Practise on photos of your own things, such as a bag, a book or a plate of food. Do not practise on a shop's photos before they agree.
  3. Learn three things first: brightness, crop and a clean background. These make the biggest difference.
  4. Use the photo fixes list below. Go through it for each photo until it becomes a habit.
  5. Keep the real product colour. If the original photo looks different from the real item, ask the shop owner for a better one.

Step 3. Make a free sample of 3 photos, before and after

  1. Choose one shop and ask for three of its product photos. The message below can help.
  2. Edit the three photos with the same look: same brightness, same crop, same background style.
  3. Put each original and edited photo side by side. Label them "Before" and "After".
  4. Send the three pairs to the shop owner. Do not post the shop's photos anywhere without their permission.

Doing the sample free shows your work without asking the shop to trust you first. It is a small cost of your own time, so start with one or two shops only.

Step 4. Set a fair price

Your price must be fair for a small shop. Look at what other freelancers or small studios charge near you before you decide. Say the price per photo in your first message, so nobody is surprised later.

Example only: ₹50 per edited photo × 20 photos a week = ₹1,000 a week. Your numbers will be different, and some weeks you may have fewer photos. This is not an income promise.

Step 5. Deliver fast and ask for a review

  1. Send the edited photos by WhatsApp, or share a Google Drive or Google Photos link that the shop owner can open.
  2. Keep to the time you promised. If you cannot, say so before the deadline.
  3. Keep the originals safe in a separate folder, so you can fix anything the shop asks for.
  4. A few days later, ask for an honest review or a short voice note. Use the message below.
  5. Ask if they have more photos coming. Regular work is easier than finding a new shop each time.

Write down each job in a small notebook: date, shop, number of photos, amount and payment status.

10 photo fixes to check on every photo

Go through these in order. Most photos only need four or five of them.

  1. Brightness: raise it a little if the photo is dark. Do not make it so bright that the details disappear.
  2. Straighten: use the straighten tool so the table edge or horizon is level.
  3. Crop: crop to square (1:1) or 4:5 so the product sits in the middle with a little space around it.
  4. Clean background: crop out clutter, or use the app's clean-up or background tool. Keep it simple and plain.
  5. Colour match: check the colour against the real product and adjust until they match.
  6. Remove dust and spots: use the heal or spot tool for dust, small marks and stray threads in the photo. Do not hide real flaws in the product.
  7. Sharpness: add a little sharpening so the detail shows, but not so much that the photo looks rough.
  8. Consistent look: use the same brightness, crop and background style across all photos for one shop.
  9. Export size: save at 1080 × 1080 pixels (square) or 1080 × 1350 pixels (4:5) for Instagram. Use a smaller size for WhatsApp status if the shop asks.
  10. File names: name files clearly, such as "red-kurta-1.jpg", so the shop can find them. Keep the original untouched.

8 kinds of shops that need this

These are only ideas. Look at the real pages of shops near you.

  • Clothing and boutiques

    Dresses, sarees and kurtas need good light and true colour. Match the colour to the real cloth.

  • Jewellery sellers

    Small items show every bit of dust and glare. A clean background and sharp detail help.

  • Home bakers and food sellers

    Warm, bright photos of cakes and snacks. Keep the colour of the food true.

  • Handmade crafts and gifts

    Candles, cards, pots and art. A plain background shows the work clearly.

  • Plant nurseries and flower sellers

    Plants look best in daylight. Straighten and crop so each plant is clear.

  • Stationery and gift shops

    Many small items with the same look. A consistent style makes the page look neat.

  • Small furniture and home decor shops

    Crooked room photos are common. Straighten, brighten and crop to show the item.

  • Salons and tailors

    Photos of finished work, such as hair, nails or stitching. Fix light and crop, and never change the work itself.
