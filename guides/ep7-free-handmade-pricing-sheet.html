Free Handmade Pricing Sheet

If you make candles, bracelets or greeting cards by hand, the hardest question is often "What should I charge?" This guide helps you pick one product, make five samples, work out a fair price with a simple calculator, and find your first buyers. It is free, and it is not a promise of income.

Before you start

Start small. Buy materials for a few pieces only, with money you can afford to lose. Do not borrow money to start. Nobody can tell you in advance how many pieces you will sell.

Make your own designs. Do not copy another maker's work, and do not use film, cartoon or brand logos on your products. Describe your product honestly. For example, do not call a candle "natural" or "skin-safe" unless it is true.

Candles can burn. If you make candles, work away from children, never leave hot wax alone, and tell buyers to burn them on a flat surface and never leave them unattended.

Step 1. Pick one product

Choose one product you enjoy making and can make the same way again and again. Three easy starting points:

  • Candles: small jar or tin candles. People buy them as gifts and for festivals. Safety matters, so go slowly.
  • Bracelets: beads, thread or charms. Light, cheap to post, and easy to make in different colours.
  • Greeting cards: birthday, thank-you and festival cards. Very cheap to make and easy to pack flat.

Start with one product only. You can add more once you know what sells. Ask five friends or family members, "Would you buy this? What would you pay for it?"

Step 2. Make 5 samples and photograph them in daylight

  1. Make five pieces of the same product. Make each one carefully, as if a customer will buy it.
  2. Write down what you spend on each piece: materials, and the minutes it takes you to make it. You need these numbers for the calculator.
  3. Take photos near a window in daylight. Do not use the flash.
  4. Put the product on a plain background, such as a white sheet or plain paper. Wipe it clean first.
  5. Take a front view, a close-up and one photo that shows the size, for example next to a coin or a hand.
  6. Show the real colours. A photo that looks better than the product leads to returns and unhappy buyers.

Step 3. Work out your price

A fair price covers four things: the materials, your time, your packing, and something extra for the business. Many beginners only count the materials and sell too cheap. Use this formula:

Cost per piece = materials + (minutes ÷ 60 × your hourly rate) + packing
Suggested price = cost per piece + your profit %

Your hourly rate is what you pay yourself for an hour of making. You choose it. Pick a number you feel is fair for your skill and area.

Example only: a piece sells at ₹150 and costs ₹60 to make. That leaves ₹90 on each piece. If you sold 20 pieces in a month, that would be 20 × ₹90 = ₹1,800 a month after costs. Your costs, prices and sales will be different, and some pieces may not sell. This is not an income promise.

Try your own numbers

Suggested price: ₹95

Cost per piece: ₹70 (materials ₹40 + time ₹20 + packing ₹10). With 30% on top: ₹91, rounded up to ₹95.

20 pieces at ₹95 = ₹1,900 in sales. After materials and packing, ₹900 is left. That includes pay for your own time.

Only an example. Type your own numbers to test them. Pieces that stay unsold are not counted. Check what similar makers charge before you decide. This is not an income promise.

Step 4. Find your first buyers

Start with people who already know you. They are more likely to give honest feedback and tell others.

  • Family and friends: show them the samples and ask for honest feedback on the quality and the price.
  • Neighbours: a few photos and the price on a message in your building or street group, if the group allows it.
  • WhatsApp status: post a photo each day with the price and "DM to order". Keep a simple order note.
  • Instagram: post your daylight photos and one short reel that shows how you make the piece. Put the price and how to order in the caption.

Do not message strangers again and again. Do not add people to groups without asking. One polite message is enough.

Ask for a photo or review

A few days after the piece arrives, ask the buyer for one honest line or a photo of it in their home. Only share a photo or review with the buyer's OK. Reviews help the next person trust you.

Keep a small notebook: date, buyer, what they bought, amount paid, and what they said.
