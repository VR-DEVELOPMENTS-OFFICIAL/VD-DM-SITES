Free Mehndi Pricing and Booking Sheet

If you enjoy drawing mehndi, you could take small bookings for friends, family and neighbours. This guide takes you from five simple practice designs to good photos, a fair price, a booking sheet you can copy, and your first reviews. It is the free guide for the keyword MEHNDI. It is free, and it is not a promise of income.

Before you start

Skin safety comes first. Use only good quality natural mehndi paste. Ask every customer about allergies and skin problems before you start, and do not apply mehndi if they are unsure or have a skin condition. Stop at once if the skin itches, burns or swells.

Start small. A few cones, plain paper and your own hand are enough to begin. Do not borrow money to start. Nobody can tell you in advance how many bookings you will get.

Step 1. Practise 5 simple designs

Before you take your first booking, get steady with five simple designs. Practise each one a few times on paper, then on your own hand or a family member's hand, until the lines come out smooth and even. Practise for 15 to 20 minutes a day.

  1. Dots and lines on a finger: a small, quick design that is easy to sell.
  2. A simple flower on the back of the hand: one flower with a few petals and leaves.
  3. A leaf or vine trail: a curved line with small leaves along the finger or wrist.
  4. A round design on the palm: a circle with petals and dots around it.
  5. A simple bracelet border: a neat band of lines and dots around the wrist.

Time yourself on each design. Your time is what you will use in the calculator below.

Step 2. Take good photos and build an album

Your photos are your shop window. People book the artist whose work they can see.

  • Use daylight: stand near a window or go outside in soft daylight. Avoid harsh midday sun and the flash.
  • Keep the background plain: a clean cloth, wall or table, so the design stands out.
  • Show the whole hand and a close-up: one photo of the full design and one of the fine detail.
  • Photograph a dry, darker design: the colour gets richer after the paste dries and the stain develops.
  • Keep the hand steady and the fingers relaxed: rest the hand on a cushion or a plain surface.
  • Ask permission: always ask before you post a photo of someone's hands.

Put your best 10 to 15 photos into one album or one WhatsApp catalogue. Write the design name and your price under each photo.

Step 3. Work out your price

Find out what one design costs you in mehndi paste, then choose a price that is above that cost and fair for your area. Compare with other mehndi artists near you before you decide.

Paste per design = cone cost ÷ designs per cone
Profit per design = price - paste per design

Example only, not a promise: a simple hand design at ₹100 × 10 designs = ₹1,000 a weekend in sales. If one ₹30 cone makes 6 designs, the paste costs ₹5 a design, so about ₹950 would be left after paste. Your time, travel and slow weekends are not counted. Your prices and number of bookings will be different. This is not an income promise.

Step 4. Use a booking sheet

A booking sheet keeps every detail in one place, so nothing is forgotten on the day. Fill one in for each booking, or copy the headings into a notebook or a spreadsheet.

Step 5. Share your work

Share often, show real work, and keep every post simple.

  • WhatsApp status: post one design photo a day with your price and how to book. Ask friends and family to share it with people who might need mehndi for a function.
  • Instagram: post photos from your album, and short reels of a design being drawn. Put your area and booking details in your bio.
  • Close to festivals and functions: people look for mehndi artists before weddings and festivals, so share early and tell people your free dates.

Use a clear name or a small logo on your photos, so people can find you again.

Step 6. Ask for a photo and a review

After each booking, ask the customer for a photo of the finished design and a short review. Always ask first before you post anything. A kind review from a real customer helps new people trust you.
