Free First-Client Message Template

Freelance writing means writing for other people for a fee, such as product descriptions, Instagram captions or resumes. This guide gives you a simple 5-step method and three messages you can copy, so you can reach your first client without feeling stuck.

తెలుగులో చదవండి

1. Pick one kind of writing

Do not try to offer everything. Choose one kind of writing and get good at it first. Three easy places to start:

  • Product descriptions. Short, clear text for items in a shop or an online store.
  • Instagram captions. A few lines to go with a photo, for a business page.
  • Resumes. A clean one-page resume for a student or a job seeker.

Choose the one you enjoy most and could do in about an hour. Writing it as one sentence helps: "I write [product descriptions] for [small shops and online sellers]."

2. Write 3 samples in free Google Docs

Nobody can trust you without seeing your work. Open a free Google Docs file, write three short samples of your chosen kind, and give each one a clear title. Then set each file to "anyone with the link can view" and test the link on your phone.

Be honest. Write the samples for made-up or practice businesses and call them "samples". Never say you worked for a brand if you did not.

3. Find your first client

Your first clients are usually close to you: small local shops and small online sellers, such as an Instagram page that sells clothes, snacks or crafts. Make a list of 10. Look for the ones whose descriptions or captions are short, missing or full of mistakes.

  1. Write the name and contact (shop visit, WhatsApp or Instagram message) of each business on your list.
  2. Send each one the message that fits them (see "Messages you can copy" below).
  3. Always share your sample links, so they can see your work in one tap.

A real client never needs you to pay first. If someone asks you for money before giving you work, say no and move on.

4. Set a fair price

Decide your price before anyone asks. For a start, keep it low enough to be an easy yes, but not so low that you feel unhappy doing the work. Agree the price, the deadline and the number of changes with the client before you begin.

Example only: ₹300 per 500-word article × 10 articles a month = ₹3,000. Your price and your work will be different. This is not an income promise.

5. Deliver on time and ask for a review

Send the work by the day you promised, in the way you agreed, and fix small changes quickly. A happy client is your best sample. A day after delivery, ask for one honest line:

"Hi [name], thank you for the work. Did the [descriptions / captions / resume] help you? If you have a minute, could you reply with one honest line about how it went? It will help me a lot."

Messages you can copy

Change everything in [square brackets] before you send. Keep it short and polite, and send each message to one person at a time.

1. First message to a local shop

First message: local shop

Hi [owner's name], I am [your name] and I live near [area]. I often pass your shop, [shop name].

I write short product descriptions for small businesses. Here are my samples: [link to Google Doc].

If you like them, I can write descriptions for your [items] for ₹[price] each. May I write one for [one of their products] so you can see how it looks? Thank you for your time.

2. First message to an online seller

First message: online seller

Hi [seller's name], I saw your [Instagram page / online store], [page name]. Your [product] looks lovely.

I noticed [one small, kind point, for example: some products have no description yet]. I write [product descriptions / Instagram captions / resumes], and my samples are here: [link to Google Doc].

My price is ₹[price] for [one description / one caption / one resume]. Would you like me to write one for [product] so you can see if it suits your page?

3. Follow-up when there is no reply

Wait 3 to 4 days. Send one follow-up only. After that, move on to the next business on your list.

Follow-up: no reply

Hi [name], I wrote to you on [day] about [product descriptions / captions / resumes] for [shop or page name]. I know you must be busy, so this is just a quick note.

My samples are here: [link to Google Doc]. If this is not for you right now, no problem at all. Just tell me and I will not message again. Thank you!

Sample ideas for your 3 samples

Pick three from this list, all of the same kind of writing:

  • A handmade item. A 50-word description for a candle, soap or jewellery piece.
  • A clothing item. A description of a saree or kurta: fabric, colour, size and care.
  • A grocery or snack item. A short, honest description for a pickle, sweet or spice pack.
  • Five bakery captions. Five captions for photos of cakes or snacks, each with a short call to action.
  • A salon or gym offer. One caption announcing a festival offer, with the date.
  • Before and after. Take a weak description and rewrite it, showing both versions side by side.
  • A fresher's resume. A one-page resume for a made-up student with no work experience.
  • A resume for a job change. A one-page resume for a made-up person moving into a new field.
